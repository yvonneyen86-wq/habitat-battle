# grammar
GT | Present Perfect: Experience
R | **have / has + p.p.** = an experience from birth until now  →  I **have been** to Japan before.
R | **Have you ever** + p.p.?  →  Yes, I **have**. · No, I **haven't**. · No, I **have never** tried it.
R | Time words **ever / never / already / before** go with the present perfect  →  I **have already learned** how to swim.
R | Regular p.p. = **V-ed**; irregular p.p. must be memorised  →  visit**ed**, learn**ed**, tri**ed** · **eaten**, **read**, **been**

# grammar
GT | Present Perfect: Just, Yet, Since, For
R | **just** goes between have/has and the p.p.  →  The school bell **has just rung**! Let's go home.
R | **yet** goes at the end of negatives and questions  →  I **haven't** finished my report **yet**. · **Have** you cleaned your desk **yet**?
R | **since** + a point in time · **for** + a length of time  →  **since 2024** / **since this morning** · **for three hours** / **for five days**
R | **have been to** = went and came back · **have gone to** = went and is still there  →  Sam **has gone to** the office. He isn't here.

# grammar
GT | Present Perfect vs Past Simple
R | **Past simple** = finished and cut off from now, with a clear past time  →  I **ate** sushi **yesterday**. · She **lived** here **in 2020**.
R | **Present perfect** = connected to now, with no clear past time  →  I **have eaten** sushi **before**. · She **has lived** here **since 2020**.
R | Past simple: **yesterday / last week / three days ago / in 2020 / when I was young** · Present perfect: **ever / never / already / yet / just / since / for / so far / recently**
R | Ask with **Have you ever ...?**, then ask for details in the past simple  →  Have you ever been to Taipei 101? When **did** you **go**? I **went** there last summer.

# grammar
GT | Passive Voice (I): be + p.p.
R | Active stresses the doer; the **passive** stresses what is done  →  Tom cleans the room.  →  The room **is cleaned** (by Tom).
R | Present: **am / is / are + p.p.** · Past: **was / were + p.p.**  →  The game **is played** by millions of people. · Three cats **were rescued** from the fire.
R | Three steps: object → subject · verb → **be + p.p.** · subject → **by** + doer (can be left out)  →  They built this temple. → This temple **was built** (by them).
R | Negative: **be + not + p.p.** · Question: **Be + S + p.p.?**  →  Phones **are not allowed** in class. · **Is** this book **sold** in Taiwan?
R | Use the **p.p.**, not V-ing  →  The window **was broken**. (passive) · The window was breaking. (past continuous)

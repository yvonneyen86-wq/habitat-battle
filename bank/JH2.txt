# reading
> Junior Reporter: Mia's Digital Life
> Hi! I am Ethan, a junior reporter at a junior high school in Zhubei. This week I interviewed my classmate Mia about her digital life.
> On weekdays, Mia always gets up at six thirty. She is never late for school. After school, she usually waits for the bus with her friends. On the bus, she often watches short videos on TikTok, but she seldom plays online games.
> At night, Mia sometimes chats online with her classmates. She is often tired after dinner, but she never stays up late. "I am usually in bed by ten thirty," she said. Her brother is different. He frequently stays up late, so he is always sleepy in class.
> Mia is a disciplined student. On weekends, she usually goes shopping or plays basketball. Everyone in our class says she is kind and healthy. My report: Mia's digital life is a healthy one!
RC | Who did Ethan interview this week? | his classmate Mia | his brother | his teacher
RC | What does Mia seldom do on the bus? | play online games | watch short videos | chat with friends
RC | What does "disciplined" mean in the passage? | good at following healthy rules | always sleepy | often late
RT | What time is Mia usually in bed? | Mia is usually in bed by ten thirty.
RT | How often does Mia chat online with her classmates? | Mia sometimes chats online with her classmates at night.
RT | How is Mia's brother different from Mia? | Mia's brother frequently stays up late, but Mia never stays up late.
RS | How is your digital life the same as Mia's or different from Mia's?
RS | Does Mia's brother need to change his habits? Why or why not?
# grammar
GT | Adverbs of frequency: where do they go?
R | **be + always / usually / often** → I am **always** happy on Fridays.
R | **always / often / never + verb** → I **often** watch YouTube after dinner.
R | **How often do / does** + subject + verb? → **How often does** Leo play games?
R | **Everyone / Nobody + is / likes** (singular verb) → **Everyone is** always happy on weekends.
R | **seldom / never** = not much / not at all → We **seldom** watch TV on weekdays.
G | I am never late for school.
G | My brother usually plays online games after dinner.
G | The weather in Taipei is often rainy in winter.
G | Does Leo sometimes go shopping with his friends?
G | We seldom watch TV because we have too much homework.
G | Kevin and I are always online on weekends.
G | Leo is usually hungry at seven o'clock.
# speak-write
SP | A: How often do you watch Reels? | B: I watch Reels almost every day. | A: How often do you {play online games}? | B: I seldom do that. I have too much homework. | play online games / chat online / go shopping / stay up late / watch TV
SW | I {always} {get up at six thirty} on weekdays. I am {never} {late for school}. | Write three sentences about your daily habits with adverbs of frequency.

GT | Past continuous: was / were + V-ing
R | **I / he / she / it + was + V-ing** → My mom **was cooking** dinner.

R | long action **was / were + V-ing** · short action **past simple** → I **was eating** when he **called**.

# grammar
GT | When vs While: short and long actions
R | **when + short action** (past simple) → I was taking a shower **when the doorbell rang**.
R | **while + long action** (was / were + V-ing) → **While my sister was studying**, I was playing games.
R | two long actions → **While my mom was cooking, my dad was washing** the dishes.
R | **When / While clause first + comma** → **When the earthquake happened,** we were watching TV.

# grammar
GT | Future: will vs be going to
R | **am / is / are + going to + verb** (a plan) → I **am going to visit** my grandma next Sunday.
R | **will + verb** (a promise or a decision now) → The phone is ringing! I **will get** it!
R | **I think / maybe + will** (an opinion) → I think it **will rain** tomorrow.
R | evidence now → **is going to** → Look at those dark clouds! It **is going to rain**.
R | **am not going to / won't + verb** → I **won't** stay up late tonight.

# reading
> Tag Question Ping-Pong
> Every Tuesday, Ms. Chen's class plays Tag Question Ping-Pong. Two players stand face to face. Player A reads a sentence card, and Player B hits back the tag as fast as possible. A positive statement needs a negative tag, and a negative statement needs a positive tag.
> Today, Leo played against Nina. Leo read, "You like bubble tea ..." Nina answered, "don't you?" Point for Nina! Then Nina read, "Tom went home early ..." Leo said, "doesn't he?" Ms. Chen shook her head. "Tom went home, so we need did. The answer is didn't he."
> The last card was a trap: "I'm the champion ..." Leo thought for a second. "Am not I? No, aren't I?" He was right. The score was nine to nine, wasn't it?
> Ms. Chen smiled. "Remember, always use a pronoun in the tag. Use falling intonation when you only want someone to agree. Let's play again next week, shall we?"

# grammar
GT | Tag Questions
R | **positive statement, negative tag** · **negative statement, positive tag**  →  He is your brother, isn't he? · They aren't coming, are they?
R | **be / can / will / should: repeat it in the tag**  →  She is a student, isn't she? · You can swim, can't you?
R | **ordinary verb: use do / does / did**  →  She plays tennis, doesn't she? · They went home, didn't they?
R | **Let's: shall we** · **imperative: will you** · **I'm: aren't I** · **There is: isn't there**  →  Let's go, shall we? · Open the door, will you?
R | **everyone / nobody: they** · **nothing: it** · **nobody, nothing: positive tag**  →  Nobody called, did they? · Nothing happened, did it?
